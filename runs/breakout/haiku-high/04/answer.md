## Design

**Strategy:** The trail channel gives the ball's last cell, so the ball's direction is (ball minus trail). When the ball is descending, I predict its landing column and move the paddle into the two cells that catch it. The ball's column at row 8 and the column it moves to at row 9 are both valid catches, so the target window is {L-1, L} for a rightward ball and {L, L+1} for a leftward one. When the ball is ascending, the paddle returns to the centre. When the direction is ambiguous (first step, after a brick or paddle bounce, or at a wall clamp), the network does nothing.

**Landing prediction:** Walls reflect the ball and repeat the edge column for one step, so the landing column is not a simple mirror. From the ball at (BY, BX) with direction d:
- Rightward, BX ≤ BY: L = 9 + BX − BY
- Rightward, BX > BY (wall fold): L = 10 − BX + BY
- Leftward, BX + BY ≥ 9: L = BX + BY − 9
- Leftward, BX + BY ≤ 8 (wall fold): L = 8 − BX − BY

**Hidden units (layer 1, tanh with gain 6):**
- h1[0] = sign of horizontal direction d; h1[1] = sign of vertical direction e (+1 = descending).
- h1[2] / h1[3] = fold indicators (rightward / leftward).
- h1[4..11] = signed threshold tests on E = paddle − L for each case (left and right thresholds, using the window offsets above).
- h1[12] / h1[13] = paddle is ≥6 / ≤3, used for centring.
- Units 14–31 are unused.

**Layer 2 (tanh AND gates, gain 8):** Each of the 10 units ANDs the direction, fold, descending and threshold signs for one action case (left or right, for each of the four cases, plus two centring cases). Units 10–31 are unused.

**Output:** logit_left and logit_right sum the eight-to-five gates with weight 8 and bias 40, so an inactive gate sits at 0 and an active one at about 16. The no-op logit is 8, so the network acts only when a gate fires. Up, down and fire are no-ops in this game, so they are left at logit 0.
