## Design

The network estimates where the ball will land on the paddle row and moves the paddle toward that column.

- **Coordinates from linear sums.** Each cell's weight is a linear function of its row and column, so hidden units compute ball column `bx`, ball row `by`, trail column `tx`, trail row `ty`, and paddle column `px` as linear forms. The ball velocity is `dx = bx - tx`, `dy = by - ty`.
- **Layer 1, gates.** G1 is on when the ball moves down-right, G2 when it moves down-left, and G3 when it moves up. Each is a tanh of `8·(…) − const`, so it is about ±1.
- **Layer 1, signs.** Using a 4× gain, A1 = sign(9+bx−by−px) and B1 = sign(10−bx+by−px) give the two candidate landings for down-right motion. Their minimum is the landing minus px, including the one-step sticking at a wall (the ball repeats a wall column once). C2 = sign(bx+by−9−px) and D2 = sign(8−bx−by−px) do the same for down-left, and E3 = sign(bx−px) handles upward motion.
- **Layer 2.** Seven AND-type units combine gates and signs into right and left decisions for each case.
- **Output.** Right and left logits are ±20 around a bias of 60 or 40. The no-op-like actions have bias 5, so ties and off-cases (rest, wall or brick bounces) default to no movement.
