## Design

**Strategy.** The ball's column is fully predictable: it moves one column per step and pauses one step at a wall. The paddle therefore tracks where the ball will be a few steps ahead, and parks on the landing column when the ball is coming down. I chose the look-ahead per phase so that a sticky-action slip rarely costs the ball. None of this could be run, so it is hand-checked only.

**Key trick.** The ball's column after k moves, T_k, is a function of ball column and trail column (the trail gives the direction; ball and trail in the same wall column means "moving away from the wall"). For every valid ball/trail pair it splits exactly into `a_k(ball col) + b_k(trail col)`. So `T_k − paddle` is a linear function of the inputs, and `tanh(10·(T_k − paddle))` gives −1, 0 or +1.

**First hidden layer (9 units used):**

| Unit | Computes |
|---|---|
| 0 | sign(T_0 − paddle), T_0 being the ball's current column |
| 1 | sign(T_1 − paddle) |
| 2 | sign(T_2 − paddle) |
| 3 | sign(T_3 − paddle) |
| 4 | sign of the post-bounce target `2·bx + tx − 3·p` (+ parity terms), which recovers the ball's true direction after a paddle hit, where the trail alone is ambiguous |
| 5 | regime E: ball and trail both in row 8 (just bounced) |
| 6 | regime D8: ball in row 8, trail in row 7 (landing step) |
| 7 | regime D7: ball in row 7, trail in row 6 |
| 8 | regime D6: ball in row 6, trail in row 5 |

**Second hidden layer (10 units used):** each is "regime AND sign", one for right and one for left per regime.

- **E:** follow unit 4.
- **D8:** go to the ball's column (T_0).
- **D7:** move only if the paddle is outside the catch window {T_1, T_2}.
- **D6:** go to T_2, the landing column.
- **Default (everything else):** track T_3. This turns the paddle around at a wall with a single action change and leaves it two cells ahead of the ball.

**Output.** Right and left get +20 when one of their units fires and −20 otherwise; no-op is 0; up, down and fire are at −100.

**Expected behaviour.** By my hand analysis, losing the ball usually needs about three sticky slips in a row, which I estimate at a few tenths of a percent per paddle bounce.
