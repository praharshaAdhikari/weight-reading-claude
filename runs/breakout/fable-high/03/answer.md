## Design

**Strategy.** The paddle goes to where the ball will land rather than chasing it. With the ball at column `c` moving horizontally in direction `dir`, and `N` steps left before it reaches row 8, the landing column is `fold(c + N·dir)`, where `fold` reflects off the side walls. The paddle can catch at two adjacent cells: the landing cell (ball bounces straight on) or the next cell along (ball is sent back the way it came). The target `A` is the centre of that pair, so the paddle stops at whichever cell it reaches first.

- **Known descent (ball at rows 4–8 going down):** `N = 8 − row`, which is exact because no bricks lie below row 3.
- **Everything else (ascending, just bounced, or among the bricks):** `N` is the earliest possible return, 8, 7, 6, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 for rows 0–8. Being in time for the earliest return guarantees being in time for any later one.
- **Just after a paddle bounce** the trail cannot tell a straight bounce from a reversed one; the row-8 table uses quarter-offset targets that give the right move in both cases.
- **Final step (ball at row 8, coming down):** if the paddle is already in the catch pair, it switches to the other cell with probability about 0.3. Both cells are safe, and this randomness breaks the endless brick-free loops a deterministic policy falls into. Two near-wall cells are nudged to prefer the bounce that leaves more room for the next trip.

**Hidden layer 1 (5 units used).**
- Units 0–3 are gated lookups: descent-right, descent-left, other-right, other-left. Each computes `tanh(0.8·(A(ball cell) − paddle column) + 30·gate)`.
- The gate is a sum of a ball-cell term and a trail-cell term that is exactly 0 for that unit's class and ≤ −1 otherwise. Exactly one unit is active and outputs `tanh(0.8·(A − p))`; the other three sit at −1.
- Unit 4 is +1 only on the final step (ball in row 8, trail in row 7).

**Hidden layer 2 (2 units used).** With `S` = sum of units 0–3 plus 3:
- Unit 0 ("go right") = `tanh(30·(S − θ))`, unit 1 ("go left") = `tanh(30·(−S − θ))`.
- `θ = 0.6068` normally, so the paddle stays put inside the pair and moves when outside it.
- On the final step unit 4 lowers `θ` to about 0.3814, just above `tanh(0.4)`, which produces the 30% switch.

**Output.** Right = 20 × unit 0, left = 20 × unit 1, no-op = 0, and the three other no-op actions get −40.

Nothing was run, so this is hand-checked only. I verified the gate tables and a few dozen spot states by arithmetic. A single sticky action is always recoverable; I expect the remaining deaths to come from two or three sticky actions landing in the same tight trip.
