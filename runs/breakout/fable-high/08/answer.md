## Design

**Strategy.** Rather than chase the ball, the paddle goes to where the ball will land and waits there. Because the ball moves on diagonals, the landing column is a linear function of the ball's cell once its direction is known. Let `n` be the number of steps until the ball is on row 8 heading down, and `u = ball_x ± n` the "unfolded" landing column, with wall bounces handled by reflection. Catching requires the paddle to be on `R(u)` or `R(u ± 1)`.

- **Going down:** `n = 8 − row`.
- **Going up:** `n` assumes the ball bounces at the first row where a brick could be: `n = row + 1` for rows 4–8, then 6, 7, 10, 9 for rows 3, 2, 1, 0. This is always a lower bound. When it is wrong, the ball gains at least as many steps as the target shifts, so the paddle can still get there.
- **Decision:** no-op if the paddle is on one of the two catching cells, otherwise move toward them. The two-cell dead-band absorbs a one-step sticky-action overshoot.
- **Jitter:** when the ball is on rows 0–1 and the paddle is already in place, it takes a random side-step about 30% of the time. This changes which side of the paddle the ball hits and is meant to break brick-less loops late in a round. The ball is at least 8 steps from the paddle then, so there is time to recover.

**Hidden layer 1 (19 units used).**
- Unit 0: horizontal direction, from ball and trail columns. A parity-staggered table makes the pre-activation exactly ±5, including the wall cases where ball and trail share a column.
- Unit 1: vertical direction, from ball and trail rows. Same row counts as "up" only on row 8, i.e. just after a paddle bounce.
- Units 2–17: for each of the four direction classes (down-right, up-right, down-left, up-left), four step comparators on `u − p` and `u + p`, where `p` is the paddle column. The `u + p` comparators implement the wall reflections.
- Unit 18: ball is on rows 0–1.

**Hidden layer 2 (9 units used).** Units 0–7 are "want right" and "want left" for each class: an AND of that class's comparators with the two direction units. Unit 8 copies the "ball is high" flag.

**Output.** Left and right get logit about 30 when wanted and about 0 otherwise. No-op gets 15 normally and 1.5 when the ball is high. Up, down and fire are suppressed at −40.

I could not run anything, so this is checked only by hand-tracing the opening bounces. The known weak spot is a corner-to-corner shot, which leaves one spare step and fails if two sticky repeats land on it (about 1% per occurrence).
