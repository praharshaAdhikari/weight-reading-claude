I wrote the weights by hand as a landing-point predictor; nothing was run, so the behaviour is checked only by tracing a few trajectories on paper.

## Strategy

The ball moves one column per step and so does the paddle, so chasing the ball's current column leaves no margin once a sticky action costs a step. Instead the network computes where the ball will next be on row 8 and moves the paddle there straight away.

- **Landing column.** For each ball cell and each of the four directions, the landing column is the ball's column plus (or minus) the number of steps left, folded back at the walls. Because the ball input is one-hot, this is a plain lookup table written into the weights.
- **Steps left.** Going down it is `8 - y`. Going up I assume the earliest possible return: a brick directly above, or the ceiling. If the ball actually flies longer, the target shifts by at most as many cells as the extra steps gained, so the paddle never falls behind.
- **Direction.** It is read from the ball and trail cells. A parity trick makes "same column at a wall" read as "moving away from the wall". "Same row" in the brick zone or at the ceiling is treated as moving down.
- **Just after a paddle bounce.** The ball sits on row 8 with its trail on row 8, and its direction depends on which side of the paddle it hit. Here the correct move is the sign of `9 + trail_x - 3*paddle_x`.

## Hidden units

**Layer 1 (14 units used):**
- Units 0–7: for each direction (down-right, down-left, up-right, up-left), one unit for "target > paddle" and one for "target ≥ paddle".
- Units 8–9: the same pair for the post-bounce rule.
- Unit 10: horizontal direction.
- Unit 11: "treat as moving down".
- Unit 12: "moving up".
- Unit 13: "ball and trail both on row 8".

**Layer 2 (10 units used):** each is an AND of one comparison unit with its direction gate, giving "go right" or "go left" for that case. Exactly one case is active at a time.

**Output:** right and left get ±20 from those units; no-op, up, down and fire stay at 0, so the paddle holds still when it is already on target.

And a ball caught in a corner must be followed across the whole row with one step to spare, so two sticky repeats in a row there will lose the ball; no policy can avoid that.
