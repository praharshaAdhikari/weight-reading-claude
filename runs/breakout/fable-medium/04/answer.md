**Design**

The strategy is to put the paddle where the ball will land when it is descending, and to shadow the ball's next column otherwise. Sticky actions only hurt when the wanted action changes, so the policy is built to avoid direct left↔right flips. Nothing here was run or simulated; I checked a handful of states by hand.

First layer (11 units used, gain 6, with large ±1200 "gates" that saturate a unit when its context is off):

- **Units 0–5, landing predictor (ball descending).** For a descending ball, the unreflected landing column `u = x + dx·(8−y)` is linear in the one-hot ball and trail cells. A parity offset in the weights also makes the wall-stall cases come out right. The six units are half-plane tests on `u` and paddle column `p`: `u−p≥1`, `u+p≤18`, `u+p≤−2`, `p−u≥1`, `u+p≥0`, `u+p≥20`. Combined in layer 2, these give the sign of (reflected landing column − p), including one wall reflection on either side. Each is gated by `ball_row − trail_row = 1`.
- **Unit 6:** descending indicator.
- **Units 7–8, tracker (ball rising, just hit a brick, or start state).** Sign of `next_x − p`, where `next_x = 2·ball_x − trail_x` with wall corrections. Switched off when the ball is in row 8.
- **Units 9–10, just bounced off the paddle (ball and trail both in row 8).** Sign of `ball_x + trail_x − 2p`, which moves the paddle the right way whether the ball kept going or reversed.

Second layer: unit 0 is "go right", unit 1 is "go left". The landing predictor dominates when active; otherwise the tracker or the post-bounce units decide. The output gives ±15 logits to right and left, 0 to no-op, and −20 to the other no-op actions.
