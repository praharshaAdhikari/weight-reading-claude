## Design

**Strategy: predict where the ball will land and wait there.** The paddle moves at the same speed as the ball moves sideways, so following the ball leaves no margin for error. Instead the network computes the set of paddle columns that will catch the ball and moves to the nearest end of that set early. That set is the ball's column when it reaches row 8, and the column it moves to on the next step; I call this interval [lo, hi].

**How the prediction works.** Sideways motion is a zigzag of period 20 that pauses one step at each wall. Bricks, the top wall and the paddle only flip the vertical direction (except a paddle hit on its far side, handled below). So the landing interval depends only on:
- the ball's cell,
- its horizontal and vertical direction, read from the trail,
- the number of steps n until it reaches row 8:
  - **Falling:** n = 8 − row.
  - **Rising, row ≥ 4:** n = row + 1, which assumes the next bounce is off row-3 bricks. This is the earliest possible landing; if the ball rises higher the target slides forward and the paddle can still keep up.
  - **Rising, rows 2–3:** n = 9 − row.
  - **Rising, row 1:** n = 10.
  - **Rising, row 0:** n = 9.
- **Just caught (ball and trail both on row 8):** the ball is rising, but its sideways direction depends on which side of the paddle it hit. I use a hand-made table whose left/right decision is correct for both cases.

When the trail and ball share a column, the ball is paused at a wall (column 0 means it now moves right, column 9 means left). When they share a row and the row is below 8, it has just bounced off a brick or the top and is falling. Both are folded into the gates below.

**Layer 1 (14 units, all exact integer arithmetic, scaled by 5)**
- **Comparison units (12).** Pre-activation is `lo(ball) − paddle − 0.5` for an R unit, or `paddle − hi(ball) − 0.5` for an L unit. The R unit is on when the paddle is left of the interval; the L unit is on when it is right of it.
  - The lo and hi values are 100-entry tables on the ball channel.
  - A gate term of 12·(score − max) uses linear functions of ball and trail coordinates. Example: (bx − tx) + [bx = 0] equals 1 exactly when the effective direction is right.
  - When the gate is off it adds −12 or less, which forces the unit off.
- **Unit groups.**
  - **D:** falling, 4 units (left/right direction × R/L).
  - **U:** rising, 4 units.
  - **B:** gated on horizontal direction only, 4 units. Used for bounce states, where the ball and trail are on the same row.
- **Unit 0, `down_ind`:** the ball is moving down (diagonally).
- **Unit 13, `up_ind`:** the ball is moving up (diagonally).

**Layer 2**
- **GoR** is computed as 3·(D/U R units) + (B R units) − 2·(indicators) − 0.5. If the ball is moving diagonally, the D/U units decide; otherwise the B units decide.
- **GoL** is the same with the L units.
- The margins are ±3.

**Output.** Right = 12·GoR and Left = 12·GoL. No-op has logit 0, which is chosen when the paddle is inside the interval. Up, down and fire are suppressed with a −15 bias.
